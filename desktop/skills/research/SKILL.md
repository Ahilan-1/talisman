# Research

Read `how_to_research.md` in this folder for the full method.

## What good research looks like

- Two independent sources for any number that matters. One source is a claim; two is a finding.
- The cheapest, fastest, earliest or best option named explicitly, with the number attached.
- A one-line reason for the recommendation, in their terms ("saves 40 minutes, costs ₹900 more").
- Every live claim traceable to a site you actually opened in this session.

## Method

1. Restate the question to yourself as a decision, not a topic: "which 6am flight is cheapest", "is the 9:40 show
   sold out".
2. Search, then open the two most authoritative results (official site, operator, venue). Ignore aggregator spam.
3. `find` the exact figure. If the page truncates, `read` the specific selector.
4. Note the timestamp: prices and seats move. Say "as of now" when it matters.
5. Answer with the recommendation first, then the comparison in one or two lines.

## Never

- Compare two numbers from different dates without saying so.
- Present a range as a price.
- Say "I could not find anything" when a site blocked you — say it blocked you.
